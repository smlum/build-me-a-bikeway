🚴 Build me a Bikeway

About

Montreal doesn't have a formal way to request new cycling infrastructure.

There's no form to fill, no official channel. The most reliable path is still the oldest one: contact your local elected official directly and make the case yourself.

This tool helps you do that — find your road, get your representative's contact, and send a clear request in a couple of minutes.

How it works

  1. Click any road on the map
  2. We find your borough's elected representative
  3. Choose the infrastructure you'd like to see
  4. Copy the draft email and send it

What else can I do?

Make it personal. The most effective requests are specific. Why do you use this road? What happened there? A personal story is harder to dismiss than a generic ask.

Appreciate what's working. If your borough has built good infrastructure, say so. Positive feedback matters — and signals you're paying attention.

Show up at borough council. Montreal boroughs hold public meetings where residents can speak. In-person carries real weight.

Come with numbers. Coordinate with neighbours so multiple people write about the same road. Collective pressure is much harder to ignore.

Connect with local groups. Vélo Québec and neighbourhood cycling groups run active campaigns. Your request gains weight as part of a collective voice.

Document the problem. Photos of dangerous conditions, routes you avoid, near-misses — concrete evidence makes requests harder to brush off.

Reading the map

Coloured lines show what already exists. Use them to spot the gaps — roads where nothing has been built yet.

Infrastructure types

Protected lane — separated from traffic by a physical barrier. The safest option.

Painted bike lane — a dedicated lane marked on the road surface.

Local street bikeway — a calmed residential street where cyclists have priority.

Multi-use path — a shared path for cyclists and pedestrians, usually off-road.
